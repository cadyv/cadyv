Cady van Assendelft

Physics Ph.D., Stanford University (defended 2026) · Quantum sensing · Science communication

I recently defended my Ph.D. in physics at Stanford, where I designed, fabricated, and tested superconducting quantum devices for low-frequency electromagnetic sensing, with applications including dark matter axion detectors. These devices use an optomechanics interaction to measure a low-frequency signal through its modulation of a microwave resonator. By reading out the signal at this higher frequency, we can take advantage of robust quantum hardware and metrology techniques.

While my research is highly specialized, I’m driven by the broader challenge of making complex scientific topics accessible. I’ve taught physics and electronics labs at both the undergraduate and graduate levels, and for two summers I ran the physics department’s research program for 50 undergraduates. In 2026 I was a finalist in Stanford’s Three Minute Thesis competition, which meant fitting a dissertation into three minutes for a general audience. Most recently, I joined Science Outside the Lab in Washington, D.C., meeting with officials at agencies like OSTP and GAO to learn how technical evidence makes its way into federal decisions.

I’m now looking for full-time roles.
